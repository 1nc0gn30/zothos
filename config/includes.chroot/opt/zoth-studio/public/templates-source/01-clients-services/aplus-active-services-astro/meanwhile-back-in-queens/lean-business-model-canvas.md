# Meanwhile Back in Queens — Lean Business Model Canvas

Date: 2026-06-13
Source: 2026-06-12 working session transcript + live Shopify data.

## Working Thesis

> Turn Queens into something wearable and collectible: a faceless lifestyle brand that fuses documentary-grade storytelling with drop-based product campaigns, where the customer is the hero.

The business is not "content company that sells product" and not "product company that makes content." It is a lifestyle brand that deliberately lives in both and balances them.

## Problem

- Queens is the only NYC borough people name hyper-specifically ("I'm from Jamaica, Queens"), yet it has the least documented wearable identity of any borough.
- The brand's research-grade storytelling lives trapped on Instagram, where it cannot be searched, cited, or owned — even though people already cite it in research papers.
- The business runs on drops (campaign → attach product), which generates spiky demand but no regular storytelling cadence or recurring revenue.
- The founder's time is consumed by accounting, lines of credit, admin, and email follow-up — the exact work his brain is worst suited for (executive dysfunction), starving the creative direction that is his actual edge.

## Customer Segments

### Economic Customers
- **Locals (~80% of base):** people who live in NYC, mostly Queens, buying wearable identity.
- **Diaspora / former residents:** moved to other US cities (three big US markets) and the UK; sizable globally.
- **Gift buyers:** buying Queens identity for someone with a known connection to the place.
- See `customer-probability-segments.md` for the p50/p95/p99 spend cut that sits *across* these.

### Power / Distribution Partners
- **p99 buyers with taste who work inside brands** (e.g. corporate marketing budget holders) who can pull the brand into a paid collaboration.
- **Institutions:** NY Mets (done once, repeating), and prospective Queens Chamber of Commerce, QEDC, and large CPG brands wanting "drop-style marketing."

## Unique Value Proposition

> Queens, made wearable. Documentary-grade storytelling about the most specific borough in New York, attached to drops you can wear and collect.

Anthropomorphized Queens as the protagonist. Earned-media relationships already in place. A library of research-grade content the market already treats as a primary source.

## Solution

1. **Drops** — campaign-led product releases (hat = top seller, e.g. "Queensborough"; hoodies; "no place like Queens"). Story first, product attached.
2. **Owned content repository** — Instagram archive → searchable blog (own the "source on Queens" position for SEO + AI engines).
3. **Always-on newsletter** — *Queens Field Notes* on Substack (faceless, community-submitted stories, twice weekly).
4. **Robust e-commerce (2026)** — head of production hired; inventory always moving, not only campaign drops.
5. **Third place (next iteration)** — a Queens cafe extending the lifestyle brand into physical space.

## Channels

- Owned Shopify store + drops.
- Instagram (~849 posts) and the blog built from it.
- Substack discovery/growth engine.
- "Community" SMS list.
- Earned media + institutional collaborations (Mets and beyond).
- Email (Shopify email today; campaigns for p95/p99 cohorts to be built).

## Revenue Streams

- **Drop product margin** — core engine, ~$500K/yr, targeting $1M.
- **Robust e-commerce (always-on inventory)** — new for 2026.
- **Collaborations / licensing** — Mets-style brand collaborations; future licensing of drop-style marketing IP and templates.
- **Future:** Substack paid tiers / founding members; cafe / third place.

## Cost Structure

- Manufacturing / inventory (production now managed by head of production).
- Content production (documentary-grade).
- Overhead / liquidity to carry the brand beyond year-end.
- AI/system tooling (Codex Business ~$200/mo, two seats) — replaces the cost of an online business manager.
- Lines of credit servicing.

## Key Metrics

- Revenue per drop and drop-over-drop trend (watch for dead-cat bounces after peaks).
- p50 / p95 / p99 cohort sizes, LTV, and repeat behavior.
- Newsletter subscribers and inbox open/scroll behavior.
- Blog traffic + citations / AI-engine mentions ("source on Queens").
- Cash runway, burn rate, line-of-credit utilization, liquidity vs. overhead.
- AOV and discount/free-shipping leakage (flagged live as something to reconcile).

## Unfair Advantage

- Hyper-specific Queens taste and references no outsider can replicate.
- Faceless model that makes the customer the hero and the merch a tribal signal.
- Documentary-grade storytelling and a research-grade content library already treated as a primary source.
- Proven drop-style marketing IP with institutional collaboration history (Mets) — and no competitor offering an "API for drop-style marketing."
- A cultural relationship with the banking system (noted as an under-used balance-sheet edge).

## Constraints / Guardrails

- Founder executive dysfunction → the system must absorb admin, follow-up, and "what's next" decisions.
- Energy/metabolic constraints → phone-to-desktop dispatch; get outside more.
- ~6 months runway; outstanding lines of credit; must cover overhead beyond year-end → CFO agent must lead cash flow, not just tally it.
- ~30% of AI output is disposable → curate and steer.

## First Wedge

The compounding intelligence system itself: connect Shopify + Google (email/calendar/docs), segment customers, surface the p99 callable list, and stand up the newsletter + blog engines — so the next drop's creative is grounded in operations and cash flow at the same time.

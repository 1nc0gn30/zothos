# Discovery & Demo Playbook — How To Do It Better Next Time

Date: 2026-06-13
Framework: Kiran Narsu discovery & demo discipline. Retro built from the 2026-06-12 Imtiaz session.

## What The Imtiaz Session Got Right

The session closed the offer. Why it worked (keep doing this):

1. **Structure before AI.** Put a lean canvas on screen first; asked him to correct three bullets. Cheap agreement, shared truth.
2. **Connected his real data.** Trust transferred from *his* Shopify data, not our claims. "How accurate is it?" → "100%."
3. **Non-obvious framing.** Probability theory (p50/p95/p99) instead of generic personas — something he couldn't have generated himself. Created the "how did it know that" moment.
4. **Economic value in the room.** Surfaced 25 callable p99 customers with names + phones. He could act that night.
5. **Showed the compounding.** Goal-mode IG→blog + Substack strategy running on their own → the system works while he sleeps and hands off to a second seat.
6. **The demo was the close.** He moved himself from "what's your pricing" to booking a same-day follow-up.

## What To Fix Next Time

| Gap in the Imtiaz session | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Formal discovery never happened.** We demoed, but skipped the structured consultation. Jai's own note: "we still didn't do the consultation yet." | Run a *short* structured discovery *before or alongside* the demo (see below). The demo is the close, but discovery is what makes the CFO/COO grounding possible and prevents scope surprises. |
| **A lot of live debugging** (iPhone↔Mac break, dashboards not reactive). | Pre-flight the demo environment. Have a known-good script + a fallback. Don't fight a broken integration live — name it, defer it, move on (we did this well — formalize it). |
| **~30% of output was noise** (bad Substack ideas, fake-reactive dashboards). | Pre-curate the demo path. Show the 70% that lands; narrate that curation is the skill, so the noise becomes a feature of the pitch, not a flaw. |
| **Time sprawled** (multiple side quests). | Timebox. One structure, one data connection, one "wow" (the p99 list), one compounding job. Resist demoing everything. |
| **Discovery of finance was shallow** (found out live there's no service revenue, finance is in Shopify/Mercury). | Ask the finance-grounding questions up front so you scope the right integration. |

## The Discipline (Kiran principles, applied)

- Discovery is not a step; it's a discipline that runs through the whole call.
- Respect + curiosity create rapport; needs are not pain — pull threads when they reveal friction.
- Quantify the pain in TIME, COST, RISK, missed revenue.
- Don't run a generic demo before you understand their world. Here, the demo *uses their data*, which is how it stays specific.
- Leave every call with a booked next step.

## The Recommended Structure (60-75 min)

| Time | Phase | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Opening | <30 sec intro; cite the specific reason you're talking. |
| 3-8 | Agenda + structure | Put a lean canvas / problem statement up. "Tell me what's false here." Get them correcting three bullets. |
| 8-20 | **Discovery** | Talk <50%. Pull threads. Quantify in TIME/COST/RISK. Hit the must-ask blocks below. |
| 20-45 | **Demo on their data** | Connect one system live. Deliver one non-obvious insight (segmentation). Surface one economically valuable artifact (callable list / cash truth). |
| 45-55 | Show the compounding | Kick off one always-on job (content migration / report / scanner) so they see it run without them. |
| 55-65 | Insight + offer | Reflect what you saw. Present $500 + $85/hr. Anchor vs. $2,000/mo OBM and $200/mo plan. |
| 65-75 | Next step | Book the integration session + the formal discovery if not yet done. |

## Must-Ask Discovery Blocks

### Their World / Operating Model
- Walk me through how the business actually runs (drops? recurring? service?).
- What do you want your brain on, and what do you want off your plate?
- What falls through the cracks? What ruins your schedule / energy?

### Current State & Stack
- Where does your data live? (store, bank, card, accounting, email, calendar, docs, SMS/email lists)
- What have you tried — ChatGPT, hiring, tools? Why didn't it stick?

### Pain & Cost (quantify)
- How many hours/week on admin/follow-up/reporting?
- What would an OBM / COO / CFO cost you? What's the missed revenue from not seeing your best customers?
- What happens if you wait 6 months?

### The Leads/Customers That Matter (the film-director lesson)
- Of your customers/contacts, who actually picks up the phone, and how warm are they? (100 leads is meaningless; who answers and what you learn is everything.)

### Rights, Risk, Guardrails
- How much bank-access risk are you comfortable with? What needs human approval before it happens?
- Who else needs access (seats)? Who takes hand-off jobs?

## Demo Asset Kit (reusable, from the Imtiaz case study)

Prepare these so every demo is concrete, not a generic dashboard tour:
1. Lean canvas / problem-statement template (fill live).
2. Probability segmentation (p50/p95/p99) recipe for their data.
3. The "callable high-value customer" query (names + last purchase + phone).
4. Differentiated-messaging + ROI-drivers doc generators.
5. One always-on job to kick off live (content migration / scheduled report / opportunity scanner).
6. The honest-expectations script (babysitting, 30% curation, chatting-with-data-not-BI, guardrails-before-bank).

## The Honest-Expectations Script (say this, it builds trust)

> "Three things. One: this is chatting with your data, not a polished dashboard product. Two: it'll ask for permission a lot and about 30% of what it generates you'll throw away — the skill is steering and curation, and that's what you're paying me for. Three: anything touching your bank starts read-only with human approval. Inside those rails, this is the most powerful operating leverage you can buy."

## Leave-With

- A booked integration session.
- A booked (or same-session) formal discovery for finance grounding.
- One artifact they can act on tonight (the callable list).

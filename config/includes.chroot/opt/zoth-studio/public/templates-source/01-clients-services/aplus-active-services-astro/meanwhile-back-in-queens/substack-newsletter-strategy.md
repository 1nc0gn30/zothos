# Queens Field Notes — Substack Growth Engine Strategy

Date: 2026-06-13

## The Problem It Solves

The brand is drop-dependent: demand-generation spikes around launches, then silence. There is no always-on storytelling cadence and no recurring relationship with the audience between drops. Today the only email tool is Shopify email (no Kajabi, Beehiiv, etc.), and a "Community" SMS list.

## The Strategy

Build **Queens Field Notes** on Substack as the always-on storytelling, discovery, and growth engine.

Why Substack over Beehiiv/Kajabi:
- **Discovery + growth engine**, not just a sender. Substack's category surface and recommendations bring new readers; the others don't.
- The brand title *"Meanwhile Back in Queens"* is strong enough to win the **Culture** category.
- Built-in Notes (Twitter-like posting), podcast (public or private), and live — multiple formats from one home.
- Paywall optionality later (monthly, yearly, **founding member**) without re-platforming.

## Format

- **Faceless and community-sourced.** People submit Queens stories — food, experiences, business owners from the diaspora. Each issue is a rich, picture-led story. Reader scrolls ~2.5 times, gets a real story, sees you in the inbox twice a week.
- **In line with how the campaigns work** (story first, faceless, customer/community as hero).
- Tagline direction: *"Queens, the center of the world."*

## Launch Plan

1. **Start free.** Lower friction, build the list and the habit.
2. **Season one = packaged archive.** Pull ~20 of the best existing posts from the ~849-post Instagram archive, sequenced over ~10 weeks as the test season. (Use a creative brief as the steering artifact — don't let the model invent the season from scratch.)
3. **Cadence:** twice weekly. Mix long-form issues + Notes + occasional video.
4. **Switch to paid later:** monthly / yearly / founding-member tiers once the audience is forming and a founding-member hook exists (e.g. a dream collaboration).

## How It Connects To The Rest Of The System

- **Grounded in Shopify data** — the model can align story themes to what actually converts and to the p99/p95 cohorts.
- **Feeds the blog and vice versa** — the IG archive → blog work (`instagram-archive-to-blog.md`) is the same content pipeline; Substack is the distribution/discovery layer, the blog is the owned/searchable layer.
- **Two top-of-funnel engines:** Substack (relationship + discovery) and SEO/AEO via the blog (own the "source on Queens" position).

## What To Watch (the 30% rule)

Substack category ideas the model generates will be mostly bad (one suggestion in-session was literally "Queens Home Department"). The winning move is the simple, obvious one: *Meanwhile Back in Queens*, faceless, community stories, twice a week. Curate hard; don't chase clever.

## Metrics

- Subscribers and growth rate (organic discovery vs. owned promotion).
- Open + scroll-depth behavior.
- Notes/post engagement.
- Free → paid conversion (later); founding-member signups (later).
- Cross-pollination: newsletter → drop waitlist signups.
